Simulacra Americana

Every American presidential election from 1789 to 2024, replayed as it happened and rerun with one thing changed: who could vote, who lived where, what people cared about, or who was on the ballot.

How it works

  • Every election opens as it happened. The electorate is rebuilt from census records, including the adults who couldn't legally vote, each kept with the reason.
  • A calibrated statistical backbone decides the counts. An unchanged rerun reproduces each state's certified Republican, Democratic and other vote in every draw.
  • Synthetic voters, interviewed in both the real and the changed world, supply only within-person changes and quotes, never counts.
  • A what-if is one of four kinds: franchise (who can vote), population (who lives where), issue (what people care about) or candidate (who is on the ballot).
  • Every rerun carries a confidence tier, and a running disclosures log records what is assumed rather than known.

Evaluation (1920 prototype, 12 held-out states)

  • Two-party vote share RMSE 7.6 points, against 8.7 for uniform swing and 17.4 for persistence.
  • Brier score for state winners 0.052, against 0.069 for uniform swing.
  • Turnout RMSE 2.9 points (persistence 16.5); Spearman rank correlation 0.92.
  • The checks it fails are published alongside the ones it passes.
